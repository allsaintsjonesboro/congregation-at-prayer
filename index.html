Catechism — The Creed
⌄

The Second Article

And [I believe] in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord, who was conceived by the Holy Spirit, born of the Virgin Mary, suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died and was buried. He descended into hell. The third day He rose again from the dead. He ascended into heaven and sits at the right hand of God, the Father Almighty. From thence He will come to judge the living and the dead. Preschool +

What does this mean?

  1. I believe that Jesus Christ, true God, begotten of the Father from eternity, and also true man, born of the Virgin Mary, is my Lord, 1st Grade +
  2. who has redeemed me, a lost and condemned person, purchased and won me from all sins, from death, and from the power of the devil; not with gold or silver but with His holy, precious blood and with His innocent suffering and death,
  3. that I may be His own and live under Him in His kingdom and serve Him in everlasting righteousness, innocence, and blessedness, just as He is risen from the dead, lives and reigns to all eternity. 2nd Grade +

This is most certainly true.

Catechesis Notes for the Week

The Lord who redeemed us by His holy, precious blood and His innocent suffering and death is the same Lord who will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead. We confess that Jesus Christ "ascended into heaven and sits at the right hand of God the Father Almighty," not because He has abandoned His Church, but because He reigns over all things for our good. Even now, He gathers us to Himself through the preaching of His Word and the administration of His Sacraments, forgiving our sins and preserving us in the one true faith. His kingdom comes among us now, though hidden beneath the cross and received through faith rather than sight.

As the Church Year draws to its close, we lift up our eyes to the promise of Christ's return. The world sees death, decay, and the apparent triumph of evil, but the Christian confesses that these enemies have already been conquered by the death and resurrection of Jesus. What Christ has begun in us through Holy Baptism, He will bring to completion on the Last Day. He will raise our bodies, deliver us from every remnant of sin, and bring us into the fullness of His everlasting kingdom. As St. Irenaeus taught against those who denied the resurrection of the flesh, our salvation is not an escape from bodily existence but the redemption of the whole man, body and soul, through the incarnate Son of God. We therefore await that Day not with terror, as though our Lord's coming were a threat to those whom He has redeemed, but with confidence in His promise. The One who comes to judge the world is the same One who bore our judgment upon the cross. He who has made us His own will not abandon us to death, but will raise us up to live with Him forever. Until that Day, we pray with the Church of every age: "Come, Lord Jesus!" (Rev. 22:20).

Prayer for Ourselves and Others
⌄
Sunday
Pray for the joy of the resurrection among us; for the fruit of faith nourished by the Word and Sacraments.
Monday
Pray for faith to live in the promises of Holy Baptism; for one's calling and daily work; for the unemployed; for the salvation and well-being of our neighbors; for schools, colleges, and seminaries; for good government and for peace.
Tuesday
Pray for deliverance against temptation and evil; for the addicted and despairing, the tortured and oppressed; for those struggling with besetting sins.
Wednesday
Pray for marriage and family, that husbands and wives, parents and children live in ordered harmony according to the Word of God; for parents who must raise children alone; for our communities and neighborhoods.
Thursday
Pray for the Church and her pastors; for teachers, deacons, deaconesses, and other church workers; for missionaries and for all who serve the Church; for fruitful and salutary use of the blessed sacrament of Christ's body and blood.
Friday
Pray for the preaching of the holy cross of our Lord Jesus Christ and for the spread of His knowledge throughout the whole world; for the persecuted and oppressed; for the sick and dying.
Saturday
Pray for faithfulness to the end; for the renewal of those who are withering in the faith or have fallen away; for receptive hearts and minds to God's Word on the Lord's Day; for pastors and people as they prepare to administer and receive Christ's holy gifts.

"Preserve Your Word, O Savior" — LSB 658 (sts. 1, 4, 5)
Stanza 1
Preserve Your Word, O Savior,
To us this latter day,
And let Your kingdom flourish;
Enlarge Your Church, we pray.
O keep our faith from failing;
Keep hope's bright star aglow.
Let nothing from truth turn us
While living here below.
Stanza 4
Preserve Your Word and preaching,
The truth that makes us whole,
The mirror of Your glory,
The pow'r that saves the soul.
Oh, may this living water,
This dew of heav'nly grace,
Sustain us while here living
Until we see Your face.
Stanza 5
Preserve in wave and tempest
Your storm-tossed little flock;
Assailed by wind and weather,
May it endure each shock.
Stand at the helm, our pilot,
And set the course aright;
Then we will reach the harbor
In Your eternal light.

Andreas Gryphius, 1616–64
